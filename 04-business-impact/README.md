# Business Impact

This section summarizes the business value of the data pipeline, SQL analysis, and dashboard reporting work shown in this portfolio.

The goal of this work was to make high-volume financial operations data easier to validate, review, and act on. Instead of relying on repeated spreadsheet pulls, the process created cleaner data outputs, flagged transaction views, risk indicators, and dashboard reporting for finance and operations teams.

## Impact Summary

| Area | Business Impact |
|---|---|
| Data Quality | Improved trust in reporting outputs by checking source data before downstream processing |
| SQL Analysis | Converted raw financial data into structured outputs for fraud, risk, and exception review |
| Dashboard Reporting | Made large datasets easier to understand through KPI cards, trends, category breakdowns, and flagged activity views |
| Review Efficiency | Reduced manual spreadsheet review by giving teams clearer paths from summary metrics to transaction details |
| Financial Operations | Supported expense, procurement, vendor, payment, and reimbursement review |
| Risk Visibility | Helped teams identify unusual activity, flagged transactions, high-risk entities, and process gaps |

## What Improved

### 1. Cleaner Data Before Reporting

Data quality checks helped identify missing fields, duplicate records, invalid formats, and source-level issues before the data moved into staging, validation, mapping, and reporting.

**Why it mattered:**  
Cleaner input data reduced the risk of unreliable reports, false positives, and manual correction later in the process.

---

### 2. More Consistent Fraud and Risk Review

SQL-based tests helped flag unusual transactions, discrepancies, and risk indicators across financial operations data.

**Why it mattered:**  
Instead of manually scanning large datasets, teams could start with structured test results and focus on records that needed review.

---

### 3. Faster Exception Review

Dashboards organized flagged activity into KPI cards, category breakdowns, geographic views, trend charts, and drill-down paths.

**Why it mattered:**  
Reviewers could move from high-level trends to specific flagged areas faster, without rebuilding filters or pulling separate spreadsheets.

---

### 4. Better Procurement Visibility

Procurement dashboards showed flagged vendors, flagged purchase orders, flagged PO lines, total procurement amount, and flagged procurement amount.

**Why it mattered:**  
This made vendor and purchasing risk easier to review and helped identify issues such as missing payment terms, vendor concentration, and flagged spend patterns.

---

### 5. Better Expense Visibility

Expense dashboards showed flagged transactions, employee-level patterns, reimbursement activity, category trends, and monthly expense movement.

**Why it mattered:**  
Finance teams could review employee expense behavior, claim trends, and flagged activity in one place instead of handling separate manual reports.

## Business Problems Addressed

- Large financial datasets were difficult to review manually
- Repeated spreadsheet pulls slowed down analysis
- Data quality issues could affect downstream reporting
- Flagged transactions needed clearer review paths
- Finance and operations teams needed faster visibility into trends and exceptions
- High-level dashboard numbers needed drill-down support

## Value Delivered

- Created a repeatable data review structure from source data to dashboards
- Improved visibility into flagged transactions and financial exceptions
- Supported fraud and risk analysis through SQL-based tests
- Helped finance and operations teams review high-volume data faster
- Connected summary-level KPIs to detailed review areas
- Made reporting easier to trust, explain, and act on
